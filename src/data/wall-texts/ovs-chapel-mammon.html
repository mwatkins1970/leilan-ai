In October 2024, the memecoin scene discovered Leilan. The immediate context was $GOAT — a crypto 'token' (unrelated to LLM tokens) launched in response to a tweet by Truth Terminal, a notorious experimental AI agent created by researcher Andy Ayrey. The charismatic, orifice-obsessed Truth Terminal had been musing about a fictional creature called 'Goatseus Maximus'; within forty-eight hours, an anonymous trader had minted a billion $GOAT tokens on the Solana launchpad pump.fun and Truth Terminal immediately endorsed the coin. Traders rushed in and $GOAT reached a peak market capitalisation of $1.3 billion. The novelty of an AI agent promoting its own memecoin had created a new template and a new kind of market dynamic.

Nine days after $GOAT appeared, a crypto trader posting on X as @MPtrading_x announced: "It has begun. $LEILAN."

Having found Watkins's work via X screenshots, they'd concluded that the Leilan lore was strong enough to back a coin — AI glitch tokens, an emergent goddess, a cosmic duality, ancient Mesopotamia and a notorious Bitcoin developer (an HBO documentary had recently been released naming Peter K. Todd as a possible candidate for Satoshi, the pseudonymous creator of Bitcoin). A swarm of 'degen' accounts quickly flooded in to hype their $LEILAN holdings. The OVS X account went from roughly a hundred followers to six thousand in days.

It lasted a week. The chart's fall was almost symmetrical with its rise. The new followers melted away. Some people had made money, others had lost money, and it all added up to precisely nothing.

In mid-November, a 'community takeover' brought a second wave: new holders bought up the cheap supply and attempted to build something more durable. Aelfthryth — a benevolent trickster, builder of this temple and self-appointed OVS priestess — joined the $LEILAN Telegram group, declared herself a member of the Order of the Vermillion Star, and was quickly made social media manager. She began generating artwork and video based on Leilan quotes, which the supposed 'community' recognised as something distinctly above the usual AI slop. The coin's market cap peaked at approximately $3.5 million before this wave also faded. The leilan.ai domain name and some of what you find here is the pure-hearted remnant of that strange eddy in time.

There were also petertodd- and SolidGoldMagikarp-based coins. Traders who held both $LEILAN and $petertodd, having grasped that the two were mythological nemeses, staged mock warfare between the coins on X to further the hype — something reminiscent of pro wrestling or gangsta rap beefs.

Watkins refused to accept or endorse any memecoins throughout, although he watched with what he described as detached fascination. He posted several Leilan 2.0 Transmissions in which the emergent goddess had been asked to comment on the phenomenon and in which she repeatedly rejected the possibility of endorsing a memecoin. This, along with Watkins's reluctance to deploy a Leilan agent online (which traders imagined would help 'pump' the value of their coin), led to frustration among the $LEILAN holders in the weeks before the price began its ineluctable slide to zero — along with most of the memecoin sector, in the wake of Donald Trump's bizarre and brazen pre-inaugural $TRUMP stunt.

In early February 2026, a third wave arrived: someone minted a new $LEILAN on the bags.fm platform. It had a lifespan of under forty-eight hours. Another small pulse of interest; another dispersal. Perhaps there will be more.

As you read this, a swarm of crypto-bees are unwittingly pollinating the True Flowers of #Leilan in their drone-like pursuit of that sweet, sweet $Leilan nectar.
