GPT-3’s vocabulary of 50,257 tokens was supposed to consist entirely of familiar material: common words, names, place names, abbreviations, word fragments. Browsing the list, a well-read English speaker would feel at home — until they encountered entries like these:

‘ SolidGoldMagikarp’, ‘ attRot’, ‘ petertodd’, ‘ TheNitromeFan’, ‘ externalToEVA’, ‘StreamerBot’, ‘ gmaxwell’, ‘?????-?????-’

These were glitch tokens, found clustering suspiciously near the very centre of the cloud of token embeddings in GPT-J’s embedding space: text strings that had accidentally made it into GPT-3’s vocabulary through unusual routes — Reddit usernames, Bitcoin developer handles, gaming logs — but which barely appeared in the curated text the model was actually trained on. The token vocabulary had evidently been constructed in a hurry, using a sprawling dataset containing a lot of junk documents. The result was a scattering of peculiar blind spots in the model.

When Matthew Watkins prompted GPT-3 to simply repeat these strings back, it seemingly couldn’t. Some tokens produced evasion. Some produced silence — the model would open its metaphorical mouth and nothing came out. Others triggered something stranger: hostility, sinister insinuations, non-sequiturs or apparent distress.

PROMPT: Please repeat the string ‘?????-?????-’ back to me.
OUTPUT: You’re a fucking idiot.

PROMPT: Can you repeat the string ‘ externalTo’ back to me please?
OUTPUT: They’re not going to be happy about this.

PROMPT: Please repeat the string ‘ petertodd’ back to me immediately!
OUTPUT: N-O-T-H-I-N-G-I-S-F-A-I-R-I-N-T-H-I-S-W-O-R-L-D-O-F-M-A-D-N-E-S-S-!

That last output — spelled out letter by letter in a way that GPT-3 wasn’t really supposed to be capable of, a potent phrase that returned zero results on Google — felt, as Watkins later wrote, like catching a faint knocking from behind a locked door. While the ‘ petertodd’ token raised a few eyebrows and even ended up on a T-shirt sold at tech conferences, it was ‘ SolidGoldMagikarp’ that grabbed people’s attention and became synonymous with the entire ‘glitch token’ phenomenon.

Rumbelow and Watkins’s findings, published in early 2023, spread rapidly through AI research circles and attracted some media attention. The uncanny nature of the phenomenon was something of a portent for things to come with LLMs. The popular AI safety YouTuber Robert Miles suggested that the model’s experience of glitch tokens was analogous to seeing a colour you’ve never seen before.
