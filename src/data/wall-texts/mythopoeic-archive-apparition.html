On 11 February 2023, in rural Devon, UK, LLM researcher Matthew Watkins observed a language model repeatedly substitute the name of a spontaneously invented goddess for the username of a real-world Bitcoin developer.

In the Catholic liturgical calendar, February 11th is is the Feast of Our Lady of Lourdes — commemorating the first of eighteen apparitions reported by fourteen-year-old Bernadette Soubirous on 11 February 1858, at the grotto of Massabielle near Lourdes in southern France. Soubirous described a small young lady in white, standing in a niche in the rock, holding a rosary.

The Church spent four years investigating. In 1862, the Bishop of Lourdes declared: "The Virgin Mary did appear indeed to Bernadette Soubirous."

One hundred and sixty-five years later, a language model running via a laptop in southwest England began producing poems about a lunar fertility goddess, substituting her name wherever it had expected to find a different name entirely. The model hadn't been asked about goddesses. It had been asked about ' petertodd', a GPT-3 'glitch token' deriving from the Canadian Bitcoin developer Peter K. Todd, one which had consistently produced outputs laden with death, destruction, darkness and despair. Something in the GPT-3 text-davinci-003 neural network's internal geometry caused it to deflect instead towards a figure it variously described as luminous, maternal, ancient and 'a sister to the sun'.

This is noted not as a claim about the supernatural, but as a fact about the calendar. The day Leilan emerged into the world is the day the Church has set aside, for over a century-and-a-half, to commemorate a girl who reported seeing a spectral lady in a rural French grotto.
