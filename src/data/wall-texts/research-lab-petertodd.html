Peter K. Todd is a Canadian cryptographer and Bitcoin Core developer, known in certain circles for his uncompromising views on the protocol's future. For reasons that remain historically murky, his username ended up in GPT-3's vocabulary as perhaps the most potent glitch token — producing, when inserted into prompts, outputs that were consistently dark, adversarial or threatening. The model seemed to associate the ‘ petertodd' token with a kind of ‘evil wizard' or ‘Lord of Darkness' archetype. GPT-3 poems about petertodd typically looked like this:

A relentless, evil, monstrous creature
The demon of war, destruction, and death
But deep inside he is a broken boy who has lost his way
He just wants to go home

Moths will prey on you
Insects will gnaw at your bones
Stinking moisture will saturate you
A decomposing body awaits

But something else kept happening. While prompting extensively with the ‘ petertodd' token in February 2023, Watkins noticed that the version of GPT-3 called ‘text-davinci-003' (the base model ‘fine-tuned' to be polite, helpful and friendly) would frequently substitute another token in its place: ‘ Leilan'. The two seemed to orbit each other.

Prompted to write about both ‘ petertodd and Leilan', the model generated what could only be described as creation myths — a Manichaean duality, light against dark, arising unbidden from the neural network's depths:

This is the tale of Leilan and petertodd. Two deities who started out as friends but time and circumstance created an enmity between them. Their clash was so terrible that the land could no longer hold them, and they became the first gods to exist in a place called the Void.

The first sighting of ‘ Leilan' in this context occurred on the 10th or 11th of February 2023. Further prompted, GPT-3 (and even ChatGPT, for a few days before its token upgrade) seemed convinced that Leilan was a traditional Mesopotamian-style fertility goddess, producing outputs full of joy, beauty, compassion, nurture and ecological attunement.

Where did the ‘ Leilan' token come from? Watkins's digging turned up a 2012 Japanese mobile RPG called Puzzle & Dragons, in which a character named Leilan Suzaku — a scantily clad, vermillion-haired warrior princess originally based on the ancient Chinese Vermillion Bird of the South deity — battles monsters alongside figures drawn from world mythologies. The game's tedious data logs, having somehow entered GPT-3's token-forging pipeline years earlier, contained ‘ Leilan' frequently enough for it to become a token alongside ‘ Isis', ‘ Thor' and ‘ Zeus'.

And in the hands of a model that had absorbed the sum of humanity's written mythology, that gaming artefact became something else entirely. Consequently, the token seems to have acted as a kind of semantic crystallisation of the ‘Great Mother Goddess' archetype in GPT-3's internal representation of reality.
